Anatomy of the Rig

The objection is that one page for the whole app will grow cumbersome rather than efficient. That is the correct worry and it has five specific causes. Below is each cause, the mechanism that answers it, and what the screen looks like when it does.

rail + stage + dock addressed, collapsible, splittable no modals 1440 × 900 unless stated

Why single-surface apps go bad

Five causes, five mechanisms

None of these is a matter of taste. Each is a thing a page gives you for free that a single surface has to earn back deliberately — and a Rig that skips any one of them earns exactly the reputation you are worried about.

Cause 1

Nothing has a name any more

You cannot bookmark it, link to it, or press browser-back. Every move becomes an unnamed mode change, and the app forgets where you were the moment you leave. This is the single biggest reason one-surface apps feel worse than pages.

Mechanism — keep the router

The stage is addressed, not moded. #/2/17, #/box/2, #/answer, #/run/2026-08-24-box2. Back, forward, bookmarks, reload and two tabs side by side all keep working, because they are the browser's and were never ours to break. The hash router in App.tsx already does this — the Rig changes what a route paints, not whether routes exist.

Cause 2

Everything secondary becomes a modal

With no room for another screen, the fifteenth operation arrives as an overlay on top of the one surface. Then two overlays. The app becomes a stack of things covering the thing you were looking at — which is how "efficient" turns into "cumbersome" in practice.

Mechanism — drawers, never overlays

Deep operations open in the dock and push, they do not cover. Box ops, claim corrections, the retire reasons, decisions.json: each is a dock drawer that takes vertical room from the rows beneath it. The stage is never occluded, so the photograph you are judging against stays visible while you operate. The one exception stays an exception: a genuinely destructive action may still gate, per docs/DESIGN.md.

Cause 3

Fixed zones are wrong for most tasks

A 280px box rail is right when choosing a box and pure waste while answering a queue. Fixed widths are optimal for the average task and suboptimal for every actual one — and 620px of committed chrome is the exact complaint the measurements made about today's app.

Mechanism — three states per zone

Rail and dock each collapse: full, mini, gone. [ and ] cycle them; the state is per-subject, so answering remembers a mini rail while browsing remembers a full one. At its narrowest the frame commits 56px and the stage takes 1384 of 1440. The zone budget below is the arithmetic.

Cause 4

You can only ever look at one thing

Real questions in this product are comparisons. Is this Thievul the one already in box 1? Did that run's console move while I kept shooting? A page can open a second tab; a single surface that forgot to allow two panes cannot.

Mechanism — the stage splits

\ splits the stage into two independently addressed panes, and because each pane is addressed, the split itself is in the hash. Two cards, or a card and its run. Closing the split is the same key. A live run additionally never needs a pane at all — see cause 5.

Cause 5

Background work forces you to go and look

A run takes minutes to hours and outlives the request that started it. If checking it means navigating away from what you are doing, you will either not check it or lose your place. That trip, repeated, is most of the friction in today's app: three visits to a fold per box.

Mechanism — a ticker, not a destination

A live run paints one line across the bottom of the stage, from any subject. Phase, elapsed, count, and a key to expand it into the stage if you want the console. Nothing to navigate to; nothing to remember to come back from. cli/runs.py already makes a run an immutable input read from disk, so this needs no state held between requests.

The Rig is not one page. It is one frame with three addressed zones, each of which can get out of the way.

Where it still loses

What the mechanisms do not fix

Four of the five causes are answered by design. These are the ones that remain real, and they are the grounds on which to reject this.

The frame is a commitment

  • Three zones is a bet that every task decomposes into subject, content and controls. Five of the six modes above do. A sixth that does not — a wide data table, a big console — has to borrow the whole stage and looks cramped doing it.
  • Collapse states are invisible until learned. [ and ] are discoverable only from the dock caption. That is one more thing to know than a page has, and it is the cost of answering cause 3.
  • Below ~1000px the dock stops being a zone. Named in the budget table rather than designed away.

The build is large and mostly not new behaviour

  • One component tree, and every Playwright selector outside the Fulfilment spec gets rewritten. The screens' logic survives — this is a re-hosting of BoxBrowse, ReviewQueue, CaptureScreen and RunPanel into zones, not a re-implementation — but the tests do not know that.
  • docs/DESIGN.md's page-chrome section becomes inapplicable. There are no pages, so its 16px padding, 20px title and 150px rule need re-arguing as frame chrome. That is a decision entry, not an edit.
  • The Fulfiller is untouched and must stay so. #/fulfillment keeps its own route at its own floors, exactly as D5 and D31 require.

Tokens, spacing and faces are PKMNSCAN's own, locked 2026-08-12 — not re-chosen here. Archivo stands in for Cabinet Grotesk, which is Fontshare-hosted and cannot load on this host.

Wireframes are drawn to the real 1440×900 aspect. Zone widths are the proposal's; the "today" figures they are compared against were measured in a browser on 2026-08-24 against the live store.
