Riftbound card sort

All 1,067 cards in Origins, Spiritforged, Unleashed, Vendetta and Radiance, sorted by how competitive play treats them. The aim: tell which abundant cards can never rise, which are played whatever the meta, and which move with it. This is a reading of tournament play to inform your own decisions, not price advice.

How to read each group

staple In 8% or more of top decks in every window since March (counting only windows after the card's set came out), and no single legend holds more than half its decks. Demand does not depend on one deck. The 2028 rotation of Sets 1 to 5 is its ceiling, and the dates of that rotation are disputed.

meta-dependent Play rises and falls with which legends win, or is tied to one legend. Each row names what would have to change for it to rise. Rising play with a flat price is the case to watch. Falling play is a reason to sell while the price holds, unless you expect the named change.

outclassed Another legal card does the same job better and gets played. The row names it. Value is unlikely to rise unless that card is banned. Bulk or sell.

banned Banned in Standard in one of the three waves (31 Mar, 24 Jul or 18 Sep 2026). Only an unban would bring it back.

unknown The evidence is too thin to call. Most were never in a top deck, but no better card could be named either. Nothing on this page is a guess.

Where play and price part ways

You care most about common, abundant cards whose price follows play. Four patterns stand out. Bars show the share of top decks in the four windows, left to right.

Does price follow play?

Measured on weekly TCGplayer sales history for Spiritforged cards, Near Mint, from the Banchi store, against the play counts above. Small samples are named.

Rares and epics: price moves with play, in the same window only. A change in play goes with a change in price inside the same ban window (r about +0.35 to +0.39). In the next window the link is about zero, so price does not lag play. Act inside the window, not after it.
Commons and uncommons: play moves volume, not price. Their play and price show r about 0. What play changes is how fast they sell.
The release drop comes first. Epics fall to about 20 to 30% of their release-week price by week 16, played or not. Commons, uncommons and rares settle at about 55 to 85%. A falling price in the first 16 weeks says little about play.
Bans move prices, and so does the play that fills the gap. Banned cards fell 30% over the 31 Mar bans, against 10% for the rest. Cards whose play rose 5 points or more after a ban rose about 20%, but only 3 to 6 cards did, so treat that as a hint.

How this was done

Play data. 371 unique top decks from 10 official Regional Qualifiers, read as raw decklists from Riot's own Top Decks articles at playriftbound.com. Counts are main deck, champion, battlefield, rune and legend rows (a legend counts the decks it leads); the sideboard is counted apart. A card counts only decks from events after its own set came out. Each article gives the best deck for each legend plus the top 8, so the sample holds about one deck per legend per event. A share here means how widely a card is used across legends. It is not the card's share of the whole field. Houston (December 2025) came before Spiritforged and is left out. Bologna's decklists were never published as an article, so W1 is Las Vegas alone.

Sorting. Spiritforged's calls come from its first review, where four judges and a skeptic overturned 11 calls; the staple test in code now applies to it as to every set. For Origins, Unleashed and Vendetta, four judges per set, one per group of domains, read every card's rules text, cost and play counts. A second pass made each judge compare every lightly played card against the 449 cards in 3 or more top decks since March, and name the one that does the same job better. A skeptic per set then tried to refute the calls and overturned 39, most of them weak outclassed calls whose named card does a different job or is barely played itself. Last, code applied the staple test and Riot's ban list over every call. Temptation was judged this time and stays unknown: no played Chaos card does its job.

Ban history. Three waves, from Riot's own ban articles. 31 Mar: Called Shot, Draven, Vanquisher, Fight or Flight, Scrapheap, and the battlefields The Dreaming Tree, Obelisk of Power and Reaver's Row. 24 Jul: Stealthy Pursuer, The Arena's Greatest and Aspirant's Climb. 18 Sep: Ekko, Recurrent and Stacked Deck.

Known gaps

Most unknowns were never in a top deck. A card with no play is called outclassed only when a played card does the same job at an equal or better rate. Where no such card could be named, it stays unknown. Vendetta has only two windows, so it has the most unknowns after Radiance.
The latest window is one event. W4 is Los Angeles alone, 36 decks, so one deck moves a share by about 3 points. Any "rising" call that rests on W4 alone is weak. Trusty Ramhound and Sterak's Gage are two of them.
The sample cannot show a legend growing. It holds about one deck per legend per event, so a legend's share stays flat by construction. Many "what must change" lines read "legend X gains share", and this data cannot confirm that happening. Riftdecks' metashare pages could, but they refused every request.
Radiance has no play yet. It came out on 23 Oct 2026 and no qualifier has been played since. Its cards are listed with prices and the release-drop stage only. It may also push out cards sorted here.
China is not covered. Chinese events have no official decklists, so none are counted.
Reprints blur the counts. 43 card names are printed in more than one of the five sets, often as an overnumbered print in a later set. Play counts cannot tell the printings apart, and a reprint shares supply, which caps how far any one print can rise. These rows name the other sets under the card number.
One price source. Every price comes from tcgcsv.com's mirror of TCGplayer, with no second source to check against.
Rotation is disputed. Riot's rules page says rotation starts in 2028. Sheep Esports reports Sets 1 to 5 leaving then. The sources disagree on how it works.
One staple rule for every set. The staple test in code now sorts Spiritforged too, comparing exact shares. Long Sword and Boots of Swiftness were at 7.9% in W2, which the first review rounded to 8%, so they moved to meta-dependent. Guardian Angel, Switcheroo and three battlefields pass the test, so they moved to staple. Each moved card shows its old group as "was".
One more champion kept as a staple. VEN-135 passes every share test and spans 8 legends, and fails only as a champion card. The skeptic kept it, as Fizz was kept.
Many "what must change" lines are generic. The skeptics noted that many meta-dependent cards say only that their legends must win more. The play data cannot show a legend growing, so treat those lines as a placeholder.
Judged by Sonnet. The Spiritforged review ran on Opus. The other sets ran on Sonnet, by the owner's choice, with a second card-by-card pass after the first pass leaned on play counts alone.
Fizz, Trickster stays a staple on judgment. It is a champion card, but its decks span many legends, with no legend above 19%. Whether any legend may play it is unverified.
