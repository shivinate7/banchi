Rig, Line, Slab

Three ways to re-do the platform's navigation and process surface, from first principles. They differ in what the top-level unit of navigation is — a place, a stage, or a subject — and that one choice decides everything below it.

A

The Rig

unit: a place · 1 route · reopens nothing

A tool you spend hours in should never change address. Navigation is replaced by focus.

The bead is the process surface

Every box row carries one bead — open, shot, identified, answering, priced, emitted, staged, live — computed from what the store and runs/ already say. Clicking a box points all three zones at whatever that box needs next. That is the terminal's status output, rendered where the work happens.

The dock generalises the best pattern already in the app

The capture screen's 32px collapsed row — key, label, right-aligned value, opens in place — is the densest, calmest control in the product and it is used on exactly one screen. In the Rig it carries claims, candidates, facts and run steps.

Zero page titles, zero nav strip, zero folds

The rail says where you are and the dock says what is true of it, so a title repeating either is chrome with no reader. Content starts at y=0.

What it costs

  • One large component tree. Every Playwright selector except the Fulfilment spec's would be rewritten.
  • 620px of 1440 is committed before any content — correct at 1440, wrong at 1100. The rail and dock need collapse behaviour, and that is the one fold this proposal grows.
  • Reopens no decision. D31's box-walk spine survives inside the stage; D33's run panel becomes a dock state. But DESIGN.md's page-chrome numbers become unreachable as written — there are no pages — so that section needs re-arguing as frame chrome.
  • The Fulfiller cannot use it and must not: he keeps his own route, unmerged, exactly as D5 and D31 already require.
B

The Line

unit: a stage · 1 route · reopens D31 + D33

The product is a conveyor, so the interface should be the conveyor. Cards are the units; stages are the addresses.

The count is the navigation and the to-do list at once

A session opens, reads one 44px strip, and knows the state of the business: 85 shot and unidentified, 47 waiting on a human, 3 needing a price. This is the single largest thing missing from the product today.

Four stages currently share one <details> element

Identify, price, emit and import all live inside the Runs fold. The Line's whole claim is that they are stages, not a panel — and that a fold visited three times per box is the evidence.

Finishing becomes visible

Clearing a stage drops its count to zero and dims the stop; the next lights. "Am I done with this box" stops being a question answered by remembering.

What it costs

  • It demotes the box axis, and D31 is explicit that the box walk is the spine — the owner ruled that finding a card is "basically find a card in a box-based system if anything". The Line makes the box a filter chip and the stage the place. That is the opposite cut, and building it means reopening D31.
  • It contradicts D33's placement argument, that a run is "something done to the box being walked". The Line's answer is the fold; that is an argument to have, not a fact to assume.
  • Seven stops is six dead controls on a quiet day, and a bar that is mostly dim stops being read.
  • PRICE is a stage nobody has built. D9's sub-threshold disposition is answered by editing text today. A workbench for it is new surface area and needs its own argument.
C

The Slab

unit: a subject · 0 routes · 40px of chrome, forever

Navigation is overhead; addressing is not. Delete the nav, the routes, the titles and the folds — keep one line that is the address bar, the search field and the verb entry at once.

It is two sentences the repo already wrote, taken literally

DESIGN.md: "an hour in the queue is a keyboard and not a mouse." App.tsx: the owner "knows every route in this app by name." The Slab is those two facts as an architecture.

It dissolves the key collisions for free

Today r is rarity on capture and reload on review; c is capture and clear-filter; u is undo on both. With one surface consuming keys there is no second screen for a letter to mean something else on — and the ,-leader chord stops being the only global key.

The money gate survives intact and gets better

D33 requires two steps and no typing. Typing the verb renders the preflight as the whole slab; the confirm appears on the line and does not exist before the preflight has answered — which is D33's own stated cheapest honest gate.

What it costs

  • Discoverability is the whole risk. A verb you do not know is a verb you cannot reach. ? is the mitigation and it is not a complete one — the honest version ships it as a persistent affordance, not a hidden key.
  • It is furthest from what exists and most likely to be half-built. A Slab with three verbs and a fallback nav strip is worse than either end.
  • It cannot be the Fulfiller's under any reading of the constraints table — a command line is the opposite of self-evident. He keeps his route, again unmerged.
  • Like the Rig, it makes DESIGN.md's page-chrome section inapplicable rather than violated. There are no pages to title.

A recommendation, not a decision

Build A first, and steal B's stage bar into its rail.

The Rig is the largest layout change with the smallest argument to have — it reopens no decision entry, because it keeps the box as the unit and only stops paginating it. The Line is the largest claim about the product and needs two entries reopened before a line is written. The Slab has the highest ceiling and the sharpest failure mode.

A rail of boxes with beads and a bar of stages with counts are the same information at two orientations. The Rig's rail can carry the stage counts as rows — which is most of the Line's benefit without touching D31.

Not proposals

Two defects found while measuring

Both are true in the tree today, independent of everything above.

#/fulfillment renders all 682 records

A measured scroll height of 124,348px — 138 viewports — because the default list is every card in the store at his 20px floor. The store is designed for tens of thousands. The Fulfilment spec cannot see it: every constraint it asserts is still met, on each of the 682 rows.

#/inventory cuts off the card you clicked

Its photograph opens at y=482 of a 900px viewport and runs 540px tall. Both folds above it are correctly collapsed and still pay 266px of vertical rent above the screen's subject. Re-ordering that column costs nothing and is independent of every proposal here.

Tokens, spacing and faces are PKMNSCAN's own, locked 2026-08-12 — not re-chosen here. Cabinet Grotesk is Fontshare-hosted and this page's host admits only Google Fonts, so Archivo stands in for the display role; every other value is the repo's.

Light only, and deliberately: docs/DESIGN.md rules out a dark theme and a switcher for a surface with one user working under a desk lamp.
