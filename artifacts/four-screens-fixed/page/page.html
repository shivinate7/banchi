Four Screens, Fixed in Place

If the Rig is not the right way, this is. No frame, no shell rewrite, no new routes — every measured defect repaired inside the screen that has it. The research's own strongest argument against the frame is the argument for this page.

0 new routes 0 shell changes 4 screens edited measured 1440 × 900

Why the frame was the wrong unit of change

Two of the Rig's three zones already exist — and the defects happened inside them

Verified in the tree: CaptureScreen.css:151 is already grid-template-columns: var(--side-w) minmax(0, 1fr), and BoxBrowse.css:137 is already minmax(300px, 380px) minmax(0, 1fr). Both are rail-and-stage layouts today. The 240px header and the y=482 photograph happened inside them.

A frame cannot fix a stacking order. Every one of the four measured defects is a decision about what sits above what, inside a column that already exists.

So the unit of change is the screen, not the shell. What the Rig was right about survives as convention rather than as architecture — each route declares its own layout and never reflows; deep operations push rather than cover; the URL addresses the subject. Those cost nothing here because the routes already exist.

Why the worklist leaves rather than moves No high-throughput annotation tool puts the pending queue beside the item. Prodigy shows one card and the last ten decisions; Label Studio separates its Data Manager from the Label Stream entirely; Zendesk removes the queue at work time so agents "spend their mental energy on solving tickets rather than combing through the queue." The price-band sort is not lost — store/queues.py:sort_key already orders expensive-first server-side, so the operator gets the benefit without paying 3,318px to look at it.
The 1:1 inset is the one change that raises accuracy FADGI requires evaluation "at a 1 to 1 pixel ratio or 100% magnification"; Metamorfoze says the same. The human on this screen is the appeal court for a detector that was wrong on 230 of 544 cards, and 19 of 40 frames disagreed with themselves between max-edge 900 and 1200 — direct evidence sheen does not survive downscaling. Judging foil from a 5.8:1 fit-to-box render asks a person to rule on evidence the standards say has been destroyed. Costs 400×300 CSS px and no keystroke.

The capture screen

#/ WITHDRAWN · nothing to change here

I had this wrong, and the owner caught it: the rig shoots PORTRAIT. Redrawing it to 9/16 does not just fix the picture — it removes the reason for the change I proposed.

Why the pane-ratio change is withdrawn, not deferred I proposed collapsing Live to a strip so Last Capture could grow. In portrait that buys nothing, and the arithmetic is the proof: --portrait-h is min(by-height, by-width), and at 1440×900 those are 748px and 827px — height binds. The frame is already as tall as the stage allows, so freeing width beside it cannot make the stored photo any bigger. My "recover ~485px" claim was landscape arithmetic applied to a portrait rig.
What survives, and it is not a layout change The verification argument stands on its own: at 623ms — 5,778 cards/hour — nobody confirms a write by looking at an image, and the panel's stated job of making a failed write visible is a counter's job being done badly by a photograph. Capture One owns a counter for exactly this reason. The repo already computes the right instrument — "85 records at indices 1..85, zero gaps, 85 distinct capture_ids" — it just never puts it on the screen. That is the whole of item 6 now.
The lesson, since this is the second time on this screen First I drew one photo where there are two. Then I drew both landscape where the rig is portrait. Both times the source said so plainly — .capture-frame-portrait has been in the file since 2026-08-23, and the research pass even flagged that "the 16:9 waste is real only at rotation 0". I had it and did not use it. The capture screen is the one screen whose geometry I should read before drawing, not after.

What the Rig was right about

Kept as convention, not as architecture

Adopted here, costing nothing

  • Each route declares its layout and never reflows. This is radiology's hanging protocol, applied per screen instead of globally — the fix is that the width is declared, not that every screen shares one.
  • Deep operations push, never cover. The two inventory folds move to the head line rather than becoming overlays.
  • The URL addresses the subject. Already true; the repo depends on it structurally.
  • One key per candidate, no modifier, no confirm — unchanged, and now vindicated by Prodigy's ~2.9s/item against Label Studio's Ctrl+Enter.

Dropped, and why

  • The global frame. It fixes none of the four defects and rewrites every selector outside the Fulfilment spec.
  • The box rail as primary navigator. Past ~15 boxes it scrolls, its items move, and the stable-coordinate claim — the frame's whole case — is gone.
  • "One frame for the entire app." Withdrawn outright: it fails four rows of the Fulfilment constraints table at once.
  • Zone collapse on [ / ]. Nothing to collapse once each screen is sized for its own task. Revisit if a screen grows a zone.
