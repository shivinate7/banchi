## D125 — Photos crop to the card on screen

**Photographs crop to the card only where they locate or price a card.** Only two screens do that: the Home hero and the pricing worklist's row thumbnails. The mechanism is `kit/index.tsx:cropStyle`, `.bn-crop` and `POST /pipeline/crop-preview`. The whole frame stays on the screens that judge a card or check the rig. Those are `#/review` and the capture screen's undo thumbnail. `#/review`'s loupe maps the pointer to a natural pixel with no letterbox arithmetic. The undo thumbnail shows a card drifting out of the stand. The owner walked a box with the crop on four more screens and had it removed. A clipping is a defect you can measure. A moving photograph is one you can feel, and the feeling is what gets used. `#/pricing`'s drawer states `object-fit: cover` in `Pricing.css` (D38's answer). Inheriting the kit's `contain` drew bars of ground down each side.

- **Focus is derived from the reading, not a constant per window.** A window at a card's own 63/88 holds a whole card, and where the card sits varies per photograph. Only the rectangle's top tracks the card reliably. The rectangle is clamped to the frame first, because real readings overrun it. The thumbnails keep a constant, 0.34, since at 36x48 the whole card cannot fit, so something must be chosen.
- **A reading that cannot be believed is a refusal.** A rectangle running more than a sixth of the frame past its edge is not framed on. The detector once answered `[216, 1718, 1530, 3553]`, which is 1,431px past a 3840px frame. It had also missed the card's top by 427px. Cropping to it put the card further out of view than no crop would. The refusal renders like `crop_refused`: no inline style, and the element's own framing untouched. The evidence is thin, three good readings and one bad, so the guard is a floor under a known failure. A fallback spelled as a default parameter value is not a fallback. `cropStyle(crop, focus ?? undefined)` let the `= 0.34` default take over and drew a sliver in a corner. Anyone who puts a crop back on a walk needs this guard first.
- **Fetching is one machine in `app/src/cardCrop.ts`.** It takes one card per call, strictly serial, observer-gated for lists, answered once per session, with one cache. An urgent request jumps the line. The box walk's auto-repeat outruns the roughly 115ms a reading costs, and FIFO would answer the card the operator is looking at last. A screen drawing one photograph asks urgently and supersedes its own previous card.
- **The kit states the crop's specificity once.** `.bn-photo img` is (0,1,1). Every screen reaches its own photograph through a (0,2,0) selector. A crop written naively loses on `height`, so `object-fit: fill` stretches the photograph into a squashed card that passes a glance. The kit states it at (0,2,1), and the cropped state clears `aspect-ratio`. Specificity that must be re-derived at each call site is the defect.
- **The crop lands instantly.** The reading is asynchronous, so a card adopts its crop about 115ms after it draws. An eased adoption would be a swoop on every step of a walk. The ease is scoped to `.home-deck-photo`, where one photograph resolves once. A value tuned for a screen that resolves once is wrong on a screen that resolves per row. Moving that rule into a shared block hides the difference.
- **The card finder is a fine-tuned D-FINE-N on onnxruntime, with `detect_card` behind it.** A paid run and `POST /pipeline/crop-preview` both ask `geometry.locate_card` (`geometry/card_box.py`). The model takes the photograph stretched to 640x640 and answers one class. Its best score wins. A score below 0.25 answers "not found". These return `detect_card`'s answer instead: a missing model file, a missing onnxruntime, a low score, a box thinner than 0.5, or any error. The 0.5 is a pre-filter. The thinnest labeled card is 0.603. A model box that `crop_refusal` refuses, in `identify.images.prepare_located`, also falls back. That is the cut a run makes. The guard lives in `identify`, and `geometry` may not import it. The card preview (`preview_rect`) shows the whole photograph instead. That is the safety path. A failed model load is logged once to stderr and retried after 60 seconds. So a missing file at start is not a model that stays off until restart. `CardBox.method` is `dfine` or the old method's name. The model's box is the card itself, axis aligned and unpadded. The margin follows the finder. `geometry.PAD_BY_METHOD` gives `dfine` 4%, and every other method keeps `identify.images.CROP_PAD`. `crop_rect` reads it. For a listed finder it skips `detect_card`'s height correction, so the card is never padded twice. The 4% margin was measured. At 8%, the eval found 6 loose crops in 74. At 4%, it found 0.
- **Measured on 74 holdout photographs** (two drawers the model never saw). Hard failures: 4 for the model against 3 for `detect_card`. Loose boxes: 0 against 12. Mean IoU to the labeled card: 0.864 against 0.766. Cost: 57 ms a photograph on CPU, against about 50 ms. Hard failure and loose box are the eval's own bands, so these figures hold for that eval only.
- **Provenance.** The model is fine-tuned on 243 corner labels from the owner's rig photographs. The labels are committed in `geometry/model/` as `labels.json` and `labels_new.json`. No photograph is committed. `geometry/model/rebuild.py` rebuilds the data and the ONNX from them. The file is 15.5 MB and is committed on the owner's word.
- **License.** D-FINE code is Apache-2.0. That is why it won over YOLO at about the same score. The starting weights are COCO-pretrained, and their license is unstated. Check it before selling anything that ships the model.
- **Runs and previews crop the same way.** This is the owner's ruling. A measured A/B on 50 cards with 100 Haiku reads found these results. Names tie 49 of 49. Numbers tie 47 of 47, with different misses. Finish reads 43 with the model crop, against 40 with `detect_card`'s. Unknown finish is 2 against 5. `cmd_identify` cuts through `prepare_located`. The crop-retry bands and the matcher still call `detect_card`.
- **Previews ask the server for the crop (D38, the photo is sized to confirm a slot).** `GET /photo/by-card/<cid>?crop=card` answers `identify.images.preview_rect`'s region, or the stored bytes on a refusal or no box. It uses the same finder and guard as a run. One finder runs at a time, and a request never waits for it. A busy finder answers the whole photograph under a seconds-long cache. Only a real answer is kept, a crop or a guard refusal, bounded by bytes. Its ETag carries `CROP_RULE_VERSION`, so a rule change revalidates old URLs. The tile lists load their photos lazily.

It touches no capture write, sidecar or backfill. Every screen is one refusal away from what it drew before.
