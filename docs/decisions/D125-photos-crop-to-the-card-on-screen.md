## D125 — Photos crop to the card on screen

**Photographs crop to the card only where they are shown to locate or price a card, and only on the two screens that keep it: the Home hero and the pricing worklist's row thumbnails (`kit/index.tsx:cropStyle`, `.bn-crop`, `POST /pipeline/crop-preview`).** The whole frame stays where a photograph is shown to judge a card or check the rig: `#/review`, whose loupe maps the pointer to a natural pixel with no letterbox arithmetic, and the capture screen's undo thumbnail, which exists to show a card drifting out of the stand. The owner walked a box with the crop on four more screens and had it removed. A clipping is a defect you can measure and a moving photograph is one you can feel, and the second is the one being used (DEBT7 is reopened on that basis). `#/pricing`'s drawer states `object-fit: cover` in `Pricing.css` (D38's answer), since inheriting the kit's `contain` drew bars of ground down each side.

- **Focus is derived from the reading, not a constant per window.** A window at a card's own 63/88 holds a whole card, and where the card sits varies per photograph. Only the rectangle's top tracks the card reliably. The rectangle is clamped to the frame first, because real readings overrun it. The thumbnails keep a constant, 0.34, since at 36x48 the whole card cannot fit and something must be chosen.
- **A reading that cannot be believed is a refusal.** A rectangle running more than a sixth of the frame past its edge is not framed on. The detector once answered `[216, 1718, 1530, 3553]`, 1,431px past a 3840px frame, having also missed the card's top by 427px, and cropping to it put the card further out of view than no crop. The refusal renders like `crop_refused`: no inline style, the element's own framing untouched. The evidence is thin (three good readings and one bad), so it is a floor under a known failure. A fallback spelled as a default parameter value is not a fallback: `cropStyle(crop, focus ?? undefined)` let the `= 0.34` default take over and drew a sliver in a corner. Anyone who puts a crop back on a walk needs this guard first.
- **Fetching is one machine in `app/src/cardCrop.ts`:** one card per call, strictly serial, observer-gated for lists, answered once per session, with one cache. An urgent request jumps the line, because the box walk's auto-repeat outruns the roughly 115ms a reading costs, and FIFO would answer the card the operator is looking at last. A screen drawing one photograph asks urgently and supersedes its own previous card.
- **The kit states the crop's specificity once.** `.bn-photo img` is (0,1,1), every screen reaches its own photograph through a (0,2,0) selector, and a crop written naively loses on `height`, so `object-fit: fill` stretches the photograph into a squashed card that passes a glance. The kit states it at (0,2,1), and the cropped state clears `aspect-ratio`. Specificity that must be re-derived at each call site is the defect.
- **The crop lands instantly.** The reading is asynchronous, so a card adopts its crop about 115ms after it draws, and an eased adoption is a swoop on every step of a walk. The ease is scoped to `.home-deck-photo`, where one photograph resolves once. A value tuned for a screen that resolves once is different on a screen that resolves per row, and moving a rule into a shared block hides that.

- **The card finder is a fine-tuned D-FINE-N on onnxruntime, with `detect_card` behind it.** `POST /pipeline/crop-preview` asks `geometry.locate_card` (`geometry/card_box.py`). The model takes the photograph stretched to 640x640, answers one class, and its best score wins. It answers "not found" below a score of 0.25. A missing model file, a missing onnxruntime, a low score or any error returns `detect_card`'s answer: that is the safety path, and it is what the preview did before the model. `CardBox.method` is `dfine` or the old method's name. The model's box is the card itself, axis aligned and unpadded. The margin stays `identify.images.CROP_PAD`, one home.
- **Measured on 74 holdout photographs** (two drawers the model never saw). Hard failures: 4 against `detect_card`'s 3. Loose boxes: 0 against 12. Mean IoU to the labelled card: 0.864 against 0.766. Cost: 57 ms a photograph on CPU against about 50 ms. Hard failure and loose box are the eval's own bands, so the figures hold for that eval only.
- **Provenance.** Fine-tuned on 243 corner labels on the owner's rig photographs, committed in `geometry/model/` as `labels.json` and `labels_new.json`. No photograph is committed. `geometry/model/rebuild.py` rebuilds the data and the ONNX from them. The file, 15.5 MB, is committed on the owner's word.
- **Licence.** D-FINE code is Apache-2.0, which is why it won over YOLO at about the same score. The starting weights are COCO-pretrained and their licence is unstated. Check it before selling anything that ships the model.
- **Not changed.** `cmd_identify` still calls `detect_card` for a run. The preview and a run can therefore disagree on a card until a run is moved (owner's call).

It touches no capture write, sidecar or backfill. Every screen is one refusal from what it drew before.
