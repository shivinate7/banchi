# D31 — One owner-side view of stored cards

**Three routes rendered the same 767 records and the owner named it: they read as separate instances of one thing.** `#/inventory` searched by SKU and sold a copy. `#/boxes` held the registry and the layout editor, with no way into a box's contents. `#/pull` walked a box with photographs. Same store, same records, three looks. A person arrives with one question: what is in this box, and can I click it? Only the fulfilment screen answered it.

**All three collapse into `#/inventory`.** `#/boxes` and `#/pull` cease to exist as routes. Box operations (name, sections, seal, delete) live on the box header inside the browse, beside the box they operate on.

**Two modes behind a switch was rejected.** It was built first, as *Browse the boxes* or *Find a card*. The owner ruled that finding a card is search in one box-based system, not two tabs. **The box walk is the spine (box, section, card). Finding a card is search over it, not a mode beside it.** The switch is gone. D7's SKU-to-positions map stays. It is drawn for the card the walk points at. So "every copy of this card and where each one sits" belongs to the selected card. Searching narrows the walk and the box strip together. Picking a result puts you in the box, at the card, with its copies beneath it.

Two tabs kept the old screens' boundary inside the new route. That is what a merge looks like when it is done on the routing table, not on the question the screens answer. There was only one question: *where is this card*, asked from two directions.

**The fold is presentation, never a filter.** Sections collapse (box 2 holds 22 of them over 544 cards). The collapsed set does not change what the keyboard walks. Arrows, PageUp/PageDown, Home/End and the box strip still traverse the whole box. Stepping into a shut section opens it. A fold that also filtered would make the arrow keys and the scrollbar disagree about the box's contents. The fold is not persisted, for the reason D19 gives about arming: state that acts on its own must be re-established deliberately.

**Collapsed is the resting state. The control names what one press does.** Both were owner reports, and they are the same defect seen twice. The screen opened with the selected card's section already open, while the button offered `expand all`. The first press expanded, and a second press was needed to reach collapsed. The owner had to press the control twice to reach collapsed. The cause was the mark-never-hidden effect firing on the initial, automatic selection, which the operator never made. A move is now a step between two selections. Nothing opens on load, on reload, or on an upstream write.

The fold control reads `any`, not `every`. From a partial state, one press always produces the state the label names. Collapse wins the tie, because collapsed is where the walk rests.

**A search opens every section holding a match, and clearing it collapses fully.** A result you cannot see is not a result. The open rule is deliberately NOT a clause in `isOpen`. A fold made during a search has to stick. Clearing returns to the resting state, not to whatever was open before, so there is one state to learn.

**Mass-select is box-scoped and not persisted.** The selection clears on a box change, because the write it feeds is box-scoped. A selection that survives into a box it cannot apply to is a loaded gun. It survives a fold. The count stays on the status line and on every section header. A fold that could hide what a write would reach is the one dangerous combination of these two features.

**The three routes with no client half landed here.** They were the whole-box delete, the mid-box delete with its contiguous shift, and the retroactive claim corrections at card and selection level. They had full T7 coverage and no control anywhere. That gap produced `CLAUDE.md`'s route-is-not-a-feature rule. This screen makes them reachable. `app/tests/inventory.spec.ts` asserts they stay that way.

**The re-shoot control stays in the merge.** D26 put it on the pull preview on purpose. That screen's whole job is one stored photo beside its position, so the remedy is on screen when a bad photo is found. The argument is about a detail panel showing one card's photograph, not about a URL. It transfers intact to the browse's card detail. A merge that loses the control has broken D26.

**The route count.** `CLAUDE.md` said six screens and six routes. The table in `app/src/App.tsx` carried seven. The gallery is the seventh, and that file's comment says the count matters more at seven routes. The count is seven now: `#/runs` (D39) and `#/pricing` (D86) joined the five that remained (capture, review, inventory, fulfillment, gallery). This merge deleted two routes that rendered one thing. Routes added for things no route rendered do not touch that ruling. `#/inventory` still holds the walk, the card, its copies and the box's operations. The pipeline left it, and hand-pricing joined. The count is restated in `CLAUDE.md`, `docs/map.py`, `README.md`, `app/src/App.tsx` and `scripts/views.txt`. The Fulfiller's nav renders no owner routes, rather than hiding them. That rule is unchanged. It was never about how many owner routes there are.

The count was wrong in five places for the life of D39. `README.md` said five screens, `docs/map.py` said five routes, and `app/src/App.tsx`'s header said five. No check failed. `scripts/docs-audit.py` reconciles no count of anything. D18 deleted the last published count on purpose. Its argument: a verifiable fact nobody can disagree with is not load-bearing prose. That holds for a number in a report. It fails for a route count. A session reads that count to learn the shape of the product before it edits. A wrong count sends it looking for a screen that does not exist, or building one that does. A check would have to decide what counts as a screen. This entry does not open that. The defense is that this paragraph exists, and that every file restating the number names this entry.

**The Fulfiller's surface is downstream and is not a counterpoint.** The case was argued the wrong way round. `#/pull` was offered as a screen the fulfilment flow leans on, as a reason to merge less. The owner ruled that fulfiller concerns and settings are downstream of build work. They are no counterpoint to any build work. D5 makes the Fulfiller's screens a skin over what the owner's build produces, and D13 puts one truth behind both. A skin settles after the thing it presents exists. Letting it veto upstream structure lets a consumer decide the shape of what it consumes.

**What this does not license.** `app/tests/fulfillment.spec.ts` and `docs/DESIGN.md`'s nine hard constraints still pass, unchanged and unweakened. `#/fulfillment` is not merged into anything and keeps its own route. It is a different persona at a different posture. It is the one screen whose whole design is a floor. The ruling is about which arguments may decide architecture. It is not about which tests may go red.
