## D110 — A hover is an alpha, not a paint

**A hover is an alpha over its ground, so one name is one perceived step on every ground.** `--bn-hover` is white at 4% in dark and ink at 3% in light. An opaque hover has to know what it sits on, and a stylesheet rule does not. `--bn-surface-3` painted over the sidebar, the page and a panel measures ΔL* 8.67, 7.70 and 4.00. The sidebar's step reads as selection, and it competes with the accent tint two rows below. The alpha measures 3.61, 4.22 and 4.51. On the page ground the old value was lighter than the ground and effectively invisible. Judge steps in CIE L*, not hex. D50 settles the principle that feedback is the product's, and this is the value it lacked.

- **Two families, split by a fact about the element.** An alpha replaces a resting background, so it is correct only where the element rests transparent. A surface lit under the pointer (nav, brand, footer buttons, `.bn-table tbody tr`, `.bn-list-row`, `.bn-menu-item`, `.pricing-row`, screen rows) reads `--bn-hover`. An object that paints its own ground (button, chip, raised card) lifts from its own color, keeps `--bn-surface-3` or `--bn-btn-bg-hover`, and carries a `translateY(-1px)` or a shadow.
- **Dark-only hover overrides are gone,** not replaced. `.bn-nav-link`, `.capture-row` and `.capture-opt` hovered down to `--bn-surface-2`, drawn as dark holes in a panel.
- **Contrast was checked on every hovered surface,** not argued from direction. `--bn-btn-bg-hover` stays a hand-picked #262b34 in dark, since a button rests on its own paint and the alpha does not reach it.
