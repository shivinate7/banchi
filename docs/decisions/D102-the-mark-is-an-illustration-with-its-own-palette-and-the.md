## D102 — The mark is an illustration with its own palette, and the spec is its store of record

**The Banchi mark is generated from `docs/specs/logo.md`, not drawn in the app; its palettes are the one place in `app/` outside `tokens.css` that may name a color; and it is fixed dark in both themes.**

`docs/specs/logo.md` locked six marks on 2026-09-05 and nothing under `app/` had been touched. `app/src/kit/index.tsx`'s `Logo` and `app/public/favicon.svg` still drew a geometric **B** in a rounded square. This entry is the wiring of that spec into the product, and the three rulings it needed.

**The geometry is generated, never hand-written.** `scripts/build-mark.mjs` reads `docs/specs/logo/sheets/small-cut.html`, evaluates the drawing routine out of it, and writes `app/src/kit/markGeometry.ts`, `app/src/kit/markPalettes.ts` and `app/public/favicon.svg`. There is therefore exactly one implementation of the mark in this repo and the app cannot drift from the spec by being edited. The alternative — copying the paths across once and maintaining them — is the shape of defect `make docs-audit`'s `motion params` row already exists to catch: two copies of one rule, drifting for weeks, with nothing comparing them. Ten committed sheets carry byte-identical geometry, which is what makes reading one of them safe.

**Two optical cuts, chosen by size, and the small one is the one that ships.** `logo.md` §3 requires a separate cut below 64px and had never drawn it; §11 swept it at 16, 28, 32 and 44px — the sizes this app actually draws — and locked stroke 4.2 with no taper. **Every surface in this product is below that boundary**: both crash pages at 40, the Fulfiller's header at 44, the favicon at 16 to 32, and — since D120 — the shell's four brand surfaces through `Lockup`, whose bracket is this same mark, at 32 on the rail and the phone bar and at kanji 40 in the sidebar and the drawer. **This sentence listed four `Logo` call sites that no longer exist** — the sidebar and its rail at 32, the phone bar at 26, the drawer at 30 — until 2026-09-07, when D120 was already the ruling; the claim they were supporting held throughout, because the cut is chosen by `size` and every one of those figures is under 64. `DISPLAY` exists so the locked cut can be looked at on `#/gallery`, and for nothing else today.

The small cut carries **no `feTurbulence`**, and that is a finding rather than a simplification: §11 measured the marbling at those sizes and it does not merely vanish, it loses to a flat prism gradient, which reads as a lit card where the displacement map reads as mush. That answers `logo.md` §10's open question about whether a favicon pipeline would keep the filter by removing the filter from every surface that has one. It also makes the bracket a constant-width wire, which a real stroked path draws in **52 bytes against the 8,989** the variable-width taper's outlined polygon needs.

**The palettes may name colors, and this is a deliberate exception to a repo-wide rule.** CLAUDE.md says `app/src/tokens.css` is the only file in `app/` that may name a color. `markPalettes.ts` names sixty. The exception is argued rather than taken: these are an **illustration's** colors, locked by a different document, and they must *not* be theme-overridable — which is exactly what putting them in `tokens.css` would invite, on top of forcing a mirrored edit to `docs/DESIGN.md`'s token block for every hex. The rule's purpose is that the product's semantic palette has one source; the mark is not part of that palette.

**The exception is only defensible with a guard standing in its place.** `make docs-audit`'s `raw color` row cannot see this file at all — its scope is `app/src/*.css`, non-recursive, and it never opens a `.ts` or `.tsx`, so sixty hexes here would pass silently. `logo parity` reconciles every palette in `markPalettes.ts` against §9's table in both directions and fails the commit on a disagreement. An exception with no reader is how a rule stops being one.

**The six are prop-selectable and bluesteel is the default.** §9 names bluesteel "the one `Logo` and `favicon.svg` take when nothing else is specified" and calls the other five "the set, not alternates to it". Nothing in the product selects a variant today; `#/gallery` draws all six, which is the kit sheet doing its stated job of keeping a primitive from rotting unseen. `Logo` had **no** gallery specimen before this, so a change to the mark was invisible to `make screenshot`.

**Fixed dark in both themes, and the reason is size rather than taste.** The placeholder was drawn in `--bn-ink` on `--bn-bg` and inverted with the theme; the mark does not. A light-theme ground was derived and swept (`logo.md` §12) and it lost. The mark is a *lit object on a dark ground* and both of its parts are light — the chrome bracket runs `#FFFFFF` → `#8FA4B8`, the prism `#E4EEF8` → `#6086AC` — so on a near-white tile the brackets are not faint, they are invisible. §3 had already eliminated an inverted ground for the same mechanism. **What decided it was the size**: at 150px the light family is genuinely attractive, and at every size this app draws, a near-white tile on a near-white page has no silhouette. That is §3's 64px boundary arriving from a completely different direction.

**One thing this cost, recorded because it will recur.** The first version of §12 compared light against a dark row whose three gold marks were *derived* from the ground rule — which for a warm prism produces the espresso §8 explicitly rejected in favor of true black. A sheet that derives a value the file has **locked** will silently redraw a rejected option, because a derivation does not know it was overruled. §9's table is the authority for those six grounds and both the sheet and `build-mark.mjs` read it rather than re-deriving it.

**A CSS radius on this mark is a defect.** The tile is a quintic superellipse and has no straight edge near its corner, so `border-radius` clips the silhouette a second time and flattens the curve the shape is chosen for. `app/src/Fulfillment.css`'s `.ff-mark` carried `border-radius: 12px` and a `box-shadow` sized for a rounded square; it uses `drop-shadow` now, which follows the real outline.

**What is NOT settled here.** The lockup — 番地 over BANCHI — is specified in `logo.md` §3 and undrawn; the sidebar still sets "Banchi" in Manrope. The bare-bracket mark is undrawn, and is a real code change rather than a layer toggle, because the card is drawn *over* the brackets. `logo.md` §11 leaves candidate B — stroke 3.4 with the same flat prism — as unchosen rather than eliminated, in §8's sense.

**What would reopen this.** *A surface at 64px or above*, which would put the display cut into the product and make the `feTurbulence` shipping question live again. *A second brand context* that wants a variant selected by something, which would need its own argument for what the variant means. *A drawn wordmark*, which is where the lockup and this entry meet.
