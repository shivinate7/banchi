# D102 — The mark has its own palette

**The Banchi mark is generated from `docs/specs/logo.md`, not drawn in the app. Its palettes are the one place in `app/` outside `tokens.css` that may name a color. It is fixed dark in both themes.**

`docs/specs/logo.md` locked six marks. Until this entry, `app/src/kit/index.tsx`'s `Logo` and `app/public/favicon.svg` drew a geometric **B** in a rounded square. This entry wires that spec into the product, with three rulings.

**The geometry is generated, never hand-written.** `scripts/build-mark.mjs` reads `docs/specs/logo/sheets/small-cut.html`, evaluates the drawing routine out of it, and writes `app/src/kit/markGeometry.ts`, `app/src/kit/markPalettes.ts` and `app/public/favicon.svg`. There is exactly one implementation of the mark, so the app cannot drift from the spec by being edited. Copying the paths once and maintaining them is the shape of defect `make docs-audit`'s `motion params` row exists to catch: two copies of one rule, drifting, with nothing comparing them. Ten committed sheets carry byte-identical geometry, which makes reading one of them safe.

**Two optical cuts, chosen by size. The small one ships.** `logo.md` §3 requires a separate cut below 64px. §11 swept 16, 28, 32 and 44px, the sizes this app draws, and locked stroke 4.2 with no taper. **Every surface in this product is below that boundary**: both crash pages at 40, the Fulfiller's header at 44, the favicon at 16 to 32. Since D120, the shell's four brand surfaces through `Lockup` are also below it. `Lockup`'s bracket is this same mark. It sits at 32 on the rail and the phone bar, and at kanji 40 in the sidebar and the drawer. The cut is chosen by `size`, and every figure here is under 64. `DISPLAY` exists so the locked cut can be seen on `#/gallery`; nothing else uses it today.

The small cut carries **no `feTurbulence`**. That is a finding, not a simplification. §11 measured the marbling at those sizes. It loses to a flat prism gradient, which reads as a lit card where the displacement map reads as mush. It answers `logo.md` §10's open question, whether a favicon pipeline would keep the filter by removing it from every surface that has one. It also makes the bracket a constant-width wire. A stroked path draws it in **52 bytes against the 8,989** that the variable-width taper's outlined polygon needs.

**The palettes may name colors. This is a deliberate exception to a repo-wide rule.** CLAUDE.md says `app/src/tokens.css` is the only file in `app/` that may name a color. `markPalettes.ts` names sixty. The exception is argued, not taken. These are an **illustration's** colors, locked by a different document. They must *not* be theme-overridable. Putting them in `tokens.css` would invite that, and would force a mirrored edit to `docs/DESIGN.md`'s token block for every hex. The rule's purpose is one source for the product's semantic palette. The mark is not part of that palette.

**The exception is only defensible with a guard standing in its place.** `make docs-audit`'s `raw color` row cannot see this file. Its scope is `app/src/*.css`, non-recursive, so it never opens a `.ts` or `.tsx`, and sixty hexes here would pass silently. `logo parity` reconciles every palette in `markPalettes.ts` against §9's table in both directions and fails the commit on a disagreement. An exception with no reader is how a rule stops being one.

**The six are prop-selectable, and bluesteel is the default.** §9 names bluesteel "the one `Logo` and `favicon.svg` take when nothing else is specified" and calls the other five "the set, not alternates to it". Nothing in the product selects a variant today. `#/gallery` draws all six, which is the kit sheet keeping a primitive from rotting unseen. `Logo` had **no** gallery specimen before, so a change to the mark was invisible to `make screenshot`.

**Fixed dark in both themes. The reason is size, not taste.** The placeholder was drawn in `--bn-ink` on `--bn-bg` and inverted with the theme; the mark does not. A light-theme ground was derived and swept (`logo.md` §12) and lost. The mark is a *lit object on a dark ground*. Both of its parts are light. The chrome bracket runs `#FFFFFF` → `#8FA4B8`, and the prism runs `#E4EEF8` → `#6086AC`. On a near-white tile the brackets are invisible. §3 had already eliminated an inverted ground by the same mechanism. At 150px the light family is attractive. At every size this app draws, a near-white tile on a near-white page has no silhouette. That is §3's 64px boundary arriving from another direction.

**One cost, recorded because it will recur.** The first version of §12 compared light against a dark row. Its three gold marks were *derived* from the ground rule. For a warm prism, that rule produces the espresso §8 explicitly rejected in favor of true black. A sheet that derives a value the file has **locked** will silently redraw a rejected option, because a derivation does not know it was overruled. §9's table is the authority for those six grounds, and both the sheet and `build-mark.mjs` read it rather than re-deriving.

**A CSS radius on this mark is a defect.** The tile is a quintic superellipse with no straight edge near its corner, so `border-radius` clips the silhouette a second time and flattens the curve the shape is chosen for. `.ff-mark` in `app/src/Fulfillment.css` uses `drop-shadow`, which follows the real outline, not `border-radius` and a `box-shadow` sized for a rounded square.

**What is NOT settled.** The lockup (番地 over BANCHI) is specified in `logo.md` §3 and undrawn. The sidebar still sets "Banchi" in Manrope. The bare-bracket mark is undrawn. It is a real code change rather than a layer toggle, because the card is drawn over the brackets. `logo.md` §11 leaves candidate B (stroke 3.4, same flat prism) unchosen, not eliminated, in §8's sense.

**What would reopen this.** *A surface at 64px or above*, which would put the display cut into the product and make the `feTurbulence` shipping question live again. *A second brand context* that wants a variant selected by something, which would need its own argument for what the variant means. *A drawn wordmark*, which is where the lockup and this entry meet.
