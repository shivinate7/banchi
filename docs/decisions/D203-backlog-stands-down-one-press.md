## D203 — Backlog stands down by one previewed press

**Stale open orders are stood down by one explicit press, with a preview and the count in its label, over a cutoff the operator sees.** Never by a rule that runs on every fetch. `Completed - Paid` cannot join `TERMINAL_STATUSES` (`store/orders.py:is_terminal_status`), since a payment clearing does not prove a card shipped. That leaves two years of history open (513 `Completed - Paid` orders fulfilled through `#/inventory` before the orders screen existed) beside 14 live `Ready to Ship` orders.

- **The predicate is never the status string.** A candidate is an order that is open (D63's narrowing), has recorded nothing against any of its lines, and was placed before the cutoff. Zero recorded is load bearing, since a part-pulled order is live work. The 14 `Ready to Ship` orders also record zero, so a naive cutoff would sweep them. The guard is not a hard-coded status exclusion, which would reintroduce the client branching D91 refused. It is the preview's breakdown, which groups candidates by the feed's own status string with counts.
- **The write is `Ledger.close_line` with `CLOSE_SHIPPED_ELSEWHERE` and nothing else** ("it went out; this store did not track it"). Not `record_fill`: many shipped from copies still on the shelf as `identified`, and a fill would claim a copy left while it stays offered to the next buyer. It claims no copy and touches no count. The physical reconcile is `#/inventory`'s job.
- **One route, two bodies (`do_order_fetch`'s shape).** `do_order_reconcile` with `{preview: true, cutoff?}` walks the ledger and writes nothing. `{cutoff?}` closes every line of every candidate inside one `Store().write()`. `cutoff` defaults to today (UTC) and compares against the first ten characters of `placed_at`. An order with no `placed_at` is never a candidate.
- **It is idempotent by construction.** A closed line owes nothing and leaves `Ledger.unfulfilled()`, so a second press finds none and says so.
- **The reversal already exists.** `reopenOrders` (`POST /orders/close` with `undo: true`, D113) reopens the orders exactly as they were, and the receipt hands it the closed `{source, number}` pairs.
- **Mechanized in T7:** the preview writes nothing, a part-pulled order and a terminal-status order are never candidates, the press closes as `shipped_elsewhere` with the inventory table byte-identical, a second press is a no-op, and the reversal reopens. Not mechanized: whether the operator reads the breakdown above the button, since nothing can make a person look (D196's limit).
