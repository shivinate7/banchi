## D32 — Crop the card in memory only

**Identification crops to the detected card at identify time, in memory, behind `--crop`.** Never on disk. A crop written at capture is irreversible once the card is back in the box. Crop at identify time also serves photos already taken, and every other consumer keeps the full frame (the review photo a human judges foil against, the pull preview, the re-shoot comparison). Detection picks the crop (`geometry.detect_card`), never a slider: card area varies 39% to 81% across a box. A refusal (`None`) sends the whole frame, and the preflight names the refusal count.

**The crop and the max edge are one pair.** `MAX_EDGE` normalizes the long edge, not the area. Frames are aspect 0.56 and a card is 0.72, so at an unchanged 1568 the crop sends more pixels (+26% cost). Cropping saves money only when `--max-edge` comes down with it. Measured on 20 frames of box 2 (whole frame at 1568 is $0.72): crop at 1200 is $0.62 and sharper on the number, at 1100 $0.56 near parity, at 900 $0.44 and softer, at 1400 $0.76 and much sharper. The run panel therefore offers named pairs (`Measured best` 1200, `Cheapest` 900, `Whole frame` 1568), and only `Custom` exposes the raw controls, with the +26% warning. The cost estimate is void when the reading changes, so `scopeKey` carries both values.

**The cache key does not carry the crop or the max edge.** A cropped run over cards already answered reuses the old answers. The panel says so in words when the preflight reports cache hits. The remedy is a sibling hash kept out of `prompt_fingerprint`, as `rarity_fingerprint` is.

**The crop preview (`POST /pipeline/crop-preview`) is free, writes nothing and is a preview, never a receipt.** It recomputes with today's detector. The crop decides framing and the max edge decides resolution, and one picture cannot carry both. The frame shows the prepared bytes with the discarded margin dimmed, and a 1:1 window onto the same file shows resolution. There is no second file to drift from the first. The frame is drawn reduced, so 1200 and 900 look alike there, and the window is where they differ. The rectangle comes from one function, `identify/images.py:crop_rect`, shared with `card_crop`. T6 asserts they match. The number band is the registry's per game (`pipeline/games.py`), and only `pokemon` claims one. A game without one gets no band and the registry's own sentence. `rect: null` means either crop off or detection refused, and `method` says which. The preview walks one card at a time with the arrow keys and wraps, and its key listener is guarded on the event target so it never steals a text field's caret keys.
