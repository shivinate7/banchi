## D104 — Live fetch is its own guarded constant

**The operator's own live listings are fetched by a press at `MyInventory: True`, behind a second guarded constant apart from the catalog export's `False`.** They are two instructions about two documents.

- **Two literals, not a flag.** `STANDING_FILTERS` (catalog) does not move, and `LIVE_FILTERS` sits beside it. A body captured off the portal is re-captured, and a re-capture pastes over every field it touches. Two literals cannot be confused, and `make docs-audit`'s `export request` row reads both and asserts they disagree on `MyInventory` and on `ExcludeListos`, and that neither references the other. No single-dict loop could catch two near-identical dicts being folded into one.
- **`LiveScope` is its own type.** The live download spans six product lines (759 rows), while `Scope.category_id` is scalar, and looping it over the registry's three ids would silently omit three. The eleven transcribed fields are duplicated on purpose, so a re-capture of one shows as a difference from the other. The transport is shared: `_post_export` follows one redirect hop, never re-sends the cookie across a host change and refuses a chain, on the one call carrying the operator's session.
- **Every narrowing axis is the portal's all-row.** On this path a rejected all-row fails loudly (HTTP 200 with an HTML `System Error`, named `tcg_request_rejected`), and a wrong narrowing fails silently as a smaller, valid CSV. So `CategoryId`, `SetNameIds`, `RarityIds`, `ConditionIds` and `LanguageIds` are all `0`. `CategoryId: "0"` is unmeasured and cannot fail quietly. If it is refused, the per-category loop is a branch to argue, never to build speculatively.
- **`ExcludeListos` is `False` here and `True` on the catalog.** On the catalog it drops other sellers' photo listings. On My Pricing every row is the operator's own, and `True` would omit the $7,000 photo listing. Catalog exports carry an empty `Photo URL`, so that measurement does not transfer.
- **`_live_shortfall` returns `None` today and exists as the guard** for the day an axis is narrowed. It is a sentence and not a count, because the file cannot report its own omissions.
- **One fetch, two consumers.** The file is kept under `inventory/.live/`. `POST /pipeline/markdowns` and `POST /pipeline/reconcile-live` accept `fetched: <name>` in place of an upload, so marking down and reconciling act on one reading. A request carrying both is refused. The upload is a door that is always open, and a dead cookie is not a dead end. Nothing is swept, because the file is the evidence for what the store wrote off it.
- **One daily launchd job also presses it, by the owner's word** (`scripts/price-refresh-daily.py`, `make price-refresh-agent`). It is the same fetch, which reads the operator's own Pricing tab and writes nothing at TCGplayer. Its cost against the portal's throttling at one request a day is unmeasured. It calls no paid read and never sweeps the archive (D224 stands: the sweep stays a press). It changes no price. A failed or missing read is never silent: `inventory/price-refresh.json` holds how the last run ended, and `#/pricing` draws it beside the listed items that moved more than 10% since listing. T7's `price_moves` group proves the note on both outcomes and that the script reaches only the live fetch and the note writer.
- **A fetched file's mtime is its fetch time,** and `Listing.observe_live` arbitrates readings by it, so a response served from a cache is dated fresher than its contents. `do_pipeline_export` shares the exposure. The fetch holds one of `REQUEST_SLOTS = 4` for up to 120 seconds (DEBT11).

Reopen for a `CategoryId: "0"` refusal, or for the owner ruling on `ExcludeListos`, which is one constant and one audit expectation.
