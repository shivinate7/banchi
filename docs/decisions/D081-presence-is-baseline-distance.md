## D81 — Presence is a distance from the session baseline

**The card-present gate compares a frame with the watch region as it stood when the trigger was armed, not with a brightness constant.** A frame is a card when it differs from that baseline by more than `presenceK` times the session's typical still-frame difference. The difference is floored at `presenceMin`. Motion detection is a frame-to-frame difference, so it is relative and cancels the light level.

The old presence gate compared brightness with the constant 90. No constant can do that job. An empty stand read a bright quantile of 57 on one rig. A real card read 61 on another. So 38 real cards were called an empty stand, silently, against one correct refusal in the corpus. Changing the statistic (mean to p90, D19) made the same mistake one layer in. Its empty-stand numbers also came from photographs of real cards, since a refusal was read as evidence of what was on the stand. `scripts/score-trace.py contact` draws every verdict's frame as a labeled contact sheet. Only looking settles what a frame contains.

- **The gap is a population gap.** An empty stand sits 1.10 to 1.38 from its own baseline. Every card sits 17.4 to 167.4. That is a 43 times gap, where brightness gave 1.07 times. Every floor from 6 to 10 gives identical verdicts. 8.0 is the middle. Illumination drift sets it (a static scene walks to 7.94 within seconds), not noise.
- **What it gives up.** A card already at the lens when the trigger is armed becomes the baseline and is refused. One frame cannot separate the stand from the stand with a card, without a reference. The failure direction is inverted on purpose. A wrong baseline causes extra fires, visible in the strip and undone with `U`. The old gate caused silent refusals. `noCardRun` counts consecutive refusals. The capture screen renders three in a row as a sentence naming the cause and the remedy. The HUD carries a `Re-baseline` control. It is a button, not a letter. Press it after clearing the stand, with a hand off the keyboard. The letter it would want is the box field's.
- **Stillness thresholds adapt the same way.** `tLo` and `tHi` are `stillK` and `moveK` times the median frame-to-frame difference. That median covers the last 8 seconds of frames the machine already calls still. The live preview's noise floor is eleven times the stored JPEGs'. A fixed retune from 3.0 to 4.5 would have cost a rig trip to learn it. The adaptive form met or beat the live constants on every trace. `stillK` is chosen from a sweep over all traces at once. It is 2.0, in the middle of a 2.0 to 2.5 plateau. The seed reproduces the hand-tuned pair 4.50 and 8.00. So the machine boots on constants confirmed at the rig, then adapts away from them. Only frames already called still feed the estimate. Otherwise a floor learned during slow motion goes blind. The one case it cannot recover from is a session whose noise is entirely above the seeded 4.5. That case is loud. No capture is taken, and the HUD sits in `moving`.
- **Kept absolute on purpose.** `tNovel` stays 4.0. `suppressed:unchanged` counted zero over 217 verdicts, so there is no basis for a multiple. Scaling it to noise would lower it on a quiet rig and make suppression likelier. A false pass is a duplicate that `U` fixes. A false suppression is a silent loss. `refractoryMs` and `maxMoveMs` are times, not light levels.
- **The HUD shows what the machine decides on:** `dbase`, `floor`, `tlo`, `thi` and `typ`. `luma` gates nothing and stays in the trace as evidence about lighting. A trace row is `[t, d, dBase, luma]` (version 2). A scorer that read the third column as brightness would read a rig with no light.

Reopen for a card genuinely indistinguishable from the baseline (a white card on a white stand under flat light). The answer is then a second signal (edges, the stand's fixed landmarks) with a trace attached.
