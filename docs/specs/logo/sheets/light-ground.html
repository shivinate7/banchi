the light-theme ground — the derivation and the sweep

The locked mark is a dark tile in both themes. This sheet asks whether a light ground can be derived for it, and it is a real question rather than a formality: section 3 already eliminated an inverted ground, because the sheen substitutes for the top of the ground gradient but not the bottom. Everything below inherits that finding — the sheen is swept here as its own variable rather than carried over.

The ground rule is not free-handed here. Every hex on this page came out of docs/specs/logo/sheets/grounds.py, which is the one implementation of section 3’s rule, and which reproduces all twelve published ground stops exactly — three prisms, both chroma families, top and bottom. Deriving the light side with a different arithmetic than the dark side would have made the two incomparable.

Two things the derivation turned up. First, section 3 states the rule with ONE fraction and its own grounds carry two: the chroma falls to 0.60 of its top value at the bottom of the gradient. Held constant the rule misses every published bottom stop; tapered it hits all six. That parameter was never written down. Second, on the light side sRGB runs out: above L 0.94 the ground is out of gamut and the hex is a clip rather than the prism’s hue. The dark side never meets that wall. Both are marked where they appear.
