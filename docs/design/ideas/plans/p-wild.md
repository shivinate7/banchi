# p-wild

**1. Name:** The rig picks the orders

**The idea:** Run the capture rig in reverse. The Fulfiller feeds whole sections back through it, and it kicks the sold cards into a tray while the rest go back in order. Picking turns from a search into a feed, and every pass also checks where each card in the section is.

**Who it helps:** The Fulfiller, who no longer hunts through 700-card boxes. The owner, who gets a free audit of positions.

**Why now:** At 1,300 cards an hour, a 40-60 card section passes in 2-3 minutes. A busy day touches maybe a dozen sections, so a peak day's picks could take under an hour, and nobody has to read a number.

**Rough shape:**
- The day's orders become a list of sections to feed, ordered by box.
- The Fulfiller drops in a section. Sold cards land in a tray, and the rest come out in their original order.
- A card the rig expected but never saw is flagged as missing, with its last photo.
- Slabs skip the feeder and go to a short hand-pick list.

**Risk:** Each extra pass wears the cards a little and risks a jam. That is fine for bulk, and the high-value cards can be kept out.

**2. Name:** Rent the rig to strangers

**The idea:** The owner has about 3,600 cards, but the rig could photograph that many in about three hours. It is idle almost all the time. Let other small local sellers drop off pre-sorted boxes on consignment: the rig captures them, identifies them in batches and lists them, and the owner takes a cut. The bottleneck was never photography; it is cards to sell.

**Who it helps:** The owner, who gets new stock without buying it. Casual sellers, who are sitting on boxes they will never list.

**Why now:** The rig and the bulk listing already exist, so each extra box costs only the identify step. Stock is small enough that doubling it changes the business, not just a number.

**Rough shape:**
- Every card records whose box it came from, and payouts come from the order files.
- A consignor gets a read-only link: what listed, what sold, what they are owed.
- Consigned boxes live in their own boxes, so picking stays simple.

**Risk:** It brings other people's disputes, bad cards and expectations into a one-person shop.

**3. Name:** The packing table witnesses

**The idea:** Put a camera over the packing table, and make the room itself the final check. Each order is laid out, photographed, matched against the order and recorded as having left the building. That photo becomes evidence when a buyer claims they got the wrong card or nothing at all.

**Who it helps:** The owner, on high-value and graded cards. The Fulfiller, who gets a calm "this is right" without having to read card text.

**Why now:** The store already knows where each card sits, but it loses track of the card at the last step. Order volume is low enough that one photo per order costs nothing, and a single disputed slab could cost more than the camera.

**Rough shape:**
- The Fulfiller lays out the pulled cards and taps "pack". The screen shows each card as matched or not.
- A wrong or missing card is flagged before the envelope is sealed.
- The photo attaches to the order, so it can be found in seconds when a dispute comes in.

**Risk:** The match has to be fast and reliable, or the Fulfiller will learn to ignore it.

**Bet:** The packing-table camera. It is the cheapest, it adds no wear and no new partners, and it closes the one gap in "know where every card is": the moment the card leaves.
