# p-buyers

**Name:** Buyer projects ledger
**The idea:** Read the order history to guess what each repeat buyer is building, and keep that guess on the Mac only. A run of card numbers from one set suggests a set completer. Several copies of the same card suggest a deck builder. One costly card or slab suggests a collector. The ledger shows the projects across buyers: which sets people are finishing, and which deck cards sell several copies at a time.
**Who it helps:** The owner, when deciding prices and what to list next.
**Why now:** At a median of 3 orders a day, about 1,000 orders a year, a person can no longer see the patterns by eye. A program still can, and every new order makes the guesses better.
**Rough shape:**
- A "Projects" list: set X has 9 buyers completing it, with their median order size and how often they come back.
- On a buyer's row: "bought 41 of set X from us across 6 orders; numbers 1-120 missing 18."
- A tag on each sold card: set-completion sale, deck sale, or collector sale. Prices can then follow the kind of demand.
**Risk:** This only works if the order export carries a stable buyer name or id. Treat that as unknown until one real export is checked.

**Name:** Pack together, fill the gaps
**The idea:** When the same buyer has placed two orders that are not yet shipped, tell the Fulfiller to pack them in one envelope. The ledger above can also print a short thank-you insert. It lists which cards this buyer still needs from their set and says they are in stock at this seller's TCGplayer storefront. All of this stays on the platform, uses no outside contact, and offers no off-site deal.
**Who it helps:** The Fulfiller, who saves postage and a packing step. The buyer, who learns the gaps are here.
**Why now:** On peak days of 39 orders, repeat buyers often split their carts, and each extra envelope is money and time. A small seller wins return visits by being easy to finish a set with.
**Rough shape:**
- The pick screen shows a badge: "Same buyer as order 2 above: pack together." The Fulfiller sees no buyer history.
- The insert is printed with the packing slip: "You're 18 cards from finishing set X. We have 11 of them."
- The owner turns inserts on or off for each set.
**Risk:** Marketplace rules on inserts may limit what the note can say. Read the policy before the first insert ships.

**Name:** Spend identify money on demand
**The idea:** Photos are cheap and the identify step is paid. So choose the next identify batch by what buyers are asking for, not by box order. Order history gives each set's sell-through speed, and the ledger shows which sets have active completers. Boxes that are photographed but not yet identified, in those sets, go to the front of the queue.
**Who it helps:** The owner's budget and cash flow.
**Why now:** About 3,600 cards and a capture rate of 1,300 an hour mean photos will pile up faster than identify money. The order in which cards get named is now a real choice.
**Rough shape:**
- The batch picker ranks sections: "Box 4, sections 3-7: set X, 9 active completers, sells out in about 12 days."
- An estimate of what a batch costs against what it will likely sell for in 30 days.
- A "sold out fast" list of cards to buy more of.
**Risk:** With only a few orders per set, the ranking can follow noise. Show the order count beside each score.

**Bet:** Build the buyer projects ledger first. It only reads exports the owner already has, costs nothing to run, and both other directions are built on its guesses.
