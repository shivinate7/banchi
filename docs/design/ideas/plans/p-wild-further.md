# p-wild-further

Built on the packing-table witness, with both cousins folded in: one photo per order with a dispute page ready to attach, and each card's capture photo shown on the order. The idea that ties them together is that the rig already photographed the exact copy being shipped. The pack photo is checked against that same copy, not against a generic picture of the card, so it shows both identity and condition at intake and at shipping.

**1. The core loop**

The Fulfiller, at the packing table:
- Opens the next order on the iPad. The screen shows each card's capture photo and an outlined slot on a printed mat, one per card.
- Lays the pulled cards face up, one per slot, and taps "pack" (or the screen takes the photo once the mat is still).
- Each slot turns green, or it shows the problem beside the capture photo.
- When everything is green, seals and moves on. One stored photo per order, with the time, order and Fulfiller attached.

The owner, three weeks later, when a buyer says "wrong card" or "it arrived damaged":
- Finds the order by its number, from the marketplace message.
- The dispute page shows the pack photo, and beside each card, its capture photo and its crop from the pack photo, plus the ship date.
- Taps once to get an image or PDF, attaches it to the marketplace reply, and writes one line.
- Marks the outcome: refunded, upheld or returned. The page keeps it.

**2. States the screen must show**
- **All cards match:** green slots and the card count, then one large "Seal" step.
- **Wrong printing:** the slot is amber, with the two photos side by side and the difference named (for example, reverse holo against holo). It points to the right card's box, section and position.
- **Missing card:** the slot is empty, with the capture photo and the card's location. It offers to send the card to the review queue as "not found" and never drops it silently.
- **Face down or overlapping:** no verdict. It says "Flip slot 3" or "Spread cards", and nothing is marked wrong.
- **Slab:** it matches the slab's printed label, not the art, because glare defeats art matching. If the label cannot be read, the Fulfiller confirms by hand, and that confirmation is stored as manual.
- **Dispute page:** pack photo, per-card pairs, ship date, export button and outcome. It is read-only once exported.

**3. Hardware assumed, and the cheapest version**
- Assumed: an iPad on an overhead stand pointing down, one desk lamp set to cut glare, and a printed matte mat with numbered slots. The mat ends most overlap problems before any software sees them.
- Cheapest version: an iPad they already own, a stand for about $30, and a mat printed on paper. Unknown: whether the iPad camera from about 40 cm resolves a printing difference. Test that first on five known pairs.
- Later, if the iPad is needed elsewhere: a fixed USB camera on an arm, wired to the Mac.

**4. Smallest first version worth shipping**
- One pack photo per order, stored with the order, and the capture photos shown beside it on screen. A person does the comparison; there is no automatic matching yet.
- The dispute page with one-tap export.
- This alone delivers the evidence and the visual check. Automatic matching comes second, and only if the measure below shows people skipping the visual check.

**5. One measure that says it worked**
- Over four weeks, at least 95% of shipped orders have a pack photo on record. Disputes are too rare at three orders a day to measure anything sooner. Coverage is the precondition for every dispute win and every wrong card caught before sealing, so count those catches alongside it.

**6. Questions only the owner can answer**
- How many wrong-card or damage claims have there been, and what did they cost? The answer decides whether automatic matching is worth building or the human check is enough.
- Do slabs and the high-value cards always pass through the owner's hands before sealing, or does the Fulfiller seal everything?
- Is the Fulfiller comfortable with their hands and the table being photographed for every order, and are the photos kept indefinitely or for a set number of days?
