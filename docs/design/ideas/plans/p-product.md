# p-product

**1. Name:** Pick list that knows the shelf

**The idea:** Sell the "where is every card" half without the camera. A stranger uploads their marketplace inventory export and marks where each divider sits. Because the cards are already sorted by set and number, the product can work out which box and section holds each card. Each order then becomes a pick list in walking order.

**Who it helps:** Sellers with 2,000 to 30,000 cards and one helper. Their current pull sheet sorts by set name, not by where the card sits.

**Why now:** It needs no capture rig, no paid identify step and no marketplace access beyond the files it already exports. Of everything here, only this can be set up by a stranger in an evening.

**Rough shape:**
- Upload the inventory file, then name your boxes and dividers ("Box 3, section 7 starts at set X, number 112").
- Each morning, upload the orders file and get one pick list across all orders, in box and section order, with a photo hint per card.
- The helper taps "picked" or "not there." A "not there" opens a short find-it flow and flags the stock count.
- After a restock, re-mark the dividers, not every card.

**Risk:** At 3 orders a day the pain is small. The paying buyer is the seller at 20 or more a day, and they may already have a warehouse tool.

**2. Name:** Pack-time evidence for disputes

**The idea:** At packing, the helper lays each card on a marked mat and takes one photo. The system matches that photo to the order line and keeps it with a timestamp. When a buyer says "wrong card," "damaged" or "never in the envelope," the seller has the proof ready.

**Who it helps:** Any seller shipping cards worth more than a few dollars, slab sellers above all. One lost dispute on a high-value card costs more than a year of software.

**Why now:** A few high-value cards and slabs carry most of the money at risk, and the photo-matching skill already exists for intake. Sellers already feel this money loss, so they would pay for it on its own.

**Rough shape:**
- The helper opens an order and the screen says "photograph these 4 cards." Each photo turns green when it matches the right card and printing.
- A mismatch stops the pack ("this is the reverse holo, the order is the regular").
- A dispute view gives one page per order: the photos, the times and the order line, ready to attach.
- Low-value lines can skip the photo, set by a price floor.

**Risk:** It adds attention per card, against the core promise. The marketplace may weigh seller photos lightly, so the cash value is unmeasured.

**3. Name:** Intake bureau for other people's boxes

**The idea:** The rig reads about 1,300 cards an hour, but the store holds only 3,600 cards, so the rig sits idle almost all the time. Sell that spare capacity as a service. Collectors and small shops mail in sorted bulk. They get back a priced, located inventory file and photos, or the cards are consigned into this store.

**Who it helps:** Collectors with boxes they never list, and card shops whose bulk sits unsorted because staff time costs more than the cards are worth.

**Why now:** The rig and the identify step are the hard part, and they are already built and paid for. A service earns money before the software is easy enough for a stranger to install.

**Rough shape:**
- The customer fills in an intake form, ships a box and gets a tracking page.
- The owner runs the box. The customer sees the card count, the cards to review and an estimated value.
- The customer then picks one: get the files and cards back, consign at a set split, or sell the lot outright.
- Every card the customer ever sent keeps its box and section, so consigned stock stays findable.

**Risk:** Shipping, liability for loss, the marketplace's rules on consignment, and the owner's own hours. It may grow into a business the owner does not want.

**What would not sell:** The rig as a kit. A stranger will not build camera hardware, and phone scanner apps already cover casual capture. A shared card-photo library across sellers would not sell either: it needs many sellers before it pays, and the big scanner apps already have larger ones.

**Bet:** Build direction 1 first. It is the only one a stranger can set up with files they already have. It sells to the same helper-run shops that would later pay for packing evidence. The shops that use it would also become the first customers for the intake bureau.
