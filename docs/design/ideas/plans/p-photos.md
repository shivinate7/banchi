# p-photos

**Premises:** none checked. The brief told me not to read the repo, so I worked from the brief's text alone.

**1. Name:** Pay once per printing
**The idea:** Every paid identification produces a labelled photo of one printing. A new capture that closely matches one of those photos can borrow its name for free. Only truly new printings would go to the paid identify step. The archive slowly becomes the owner's own identification library.
**Who it helps:** The owner, through lower identify cost per batch, more so as the archive grows.
**Why now:** Bulk commons arrive in multiples, and sorted input means the set and number sequence is a strong hint. Every week of photos makes this better.
**Rough shape:**
- A capture run shows three counts: matched from the archive, sent to paid identify, and needs review.
- Before a batch, the owner sees the estimated cost with and without archive matches.
- A borrowed name links to the photo it matched, so the owner can check it side by side.
- Lookalike printings (reverse holo against normal, promo stamps) never borrow a name. They go to the review queue.
**Risk:** A wrong match on near-identical variants would be a guessed identification. How often variants differ only slightly is unmeasured.

**2. Name:** Re-shoot a section, see the drift
**The idea:** The archive is a dated record of what each section held. Run one section back through the rig and compare it with that record. The result shows cards that are missing, extra, out of order, or filed in the wrong section. An inventory count becomes a 5-minute machine pass instead of a hand count.
**Who it helps:** The Fulfiller, whose mispulls and misfiles get caught. Also the owner, who gets real proof of "where each card is" instead of trust.
**Why now:** About 3,600 cards and up to 39 orders a day means drift builds up quietly. Nothing checks it yet.
**Rough shape:**
- The owner picks a box and section, runs it through the rig, and gets a list of differences with a photo for each one.
- Fixes are one tap each: "re-file here", "mark lost", "this is new stock".
- Each section shows when it was last checked, and the section with the most drift is suggested next.
**Risk:** Rig time competes with new captures, and a sloppy re-shoot could report drift that is not real.

**3. Name:** The lucky copy finder
**The idea:** Group all photos of the same printing and look for the copies that differ from the rest. Miscuts, ink errors, unusual holo patterns and off-centre prints worth a premium would stand out. Round 1 covered damage, which is a downside. This is the upside: hidden value in cheap bulk.
**Who it helps:** The owner, who keeps a premium copy out of a bulk listing that sells it at the common price.
**Why now:** A pattern only shows across many copies. The archive is just reaching that size, and the rig's even lighting makes copies comparable.
**Rough shape:**
- A weekly "worth a second look" list shows each unusual copy next to a typical copy of the same card.
- The owner rules each one: normal, error, or premium. A premium copy is held from the bulk listing.
- Where marketplace rules allow photo listings for single copies (unknown for TCGplayer), the archive photo becomes the listing photo.
**Risk:** Most flags may turn out to be normal print variation, and the owner learns to ignore the list.

**Bet:** Pay once per printing. It cuts a cost that recurs on every batch. It pays more each week the archive grows, and it uses photos already taken. Its one danger, lookalike variants, already has a home: the review queue, so nothing is guessed. The saving depends on how many duplicate copies arrive, which is unmeasured. One past batch replayed against the archive would measure it before anything is built.
