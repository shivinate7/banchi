# p-subtract

**Subtraction lens: three directions to cut or merge**

**1. Name: One "Today" screen for the Fulfiller**
**The idea:** Merge Orders, Shipping and Sales History into one list of today's pulls, sorted by box, then section, then position. At 3 orders a day, moving between three screens costs more than the work itself. History stops being a screen and becomes the same list set to an earlier date.
**Who it helps:** The Fulfiller. They are not technical and should never have to choose which screen to open.
**Why now:** The median day has 3 orders and the peak has 39. One sorted list handles both. Three screens only made sense if the volume was much higher.
**Rough shape:**
- They open one page that shows "4 to pull" with the box and section in large type.
- They tap a card when it is in hand, then tap "packed", then "shipped". There are no other states.
- Past days sit behind a date arrow, not a separate tab.
**Risk:** The owner loses a dense table for finding a dispute later. A search box has to replace it.

**2. Name: The Vault and the Pile**
**The idea:** Set one value line. Cards under it (most of the 3,600) skip review and hand pricing: a single rule prices them, and an uncertain card goes into an unlisted pile, not a review queue. Cards over the line and all graded slabs go to one Vault screen, where the owner checks each by eye. This merges Review, Pricing and Slabs.
**Who it helps:** The owner. Most of their review minutes go to cards worth less than those minutes.
**Why now:** Each identify run is paid, and each review costs attention. Spending both on very cheap cards is a loss at this size.
**Rough shape:**
- One setting: the value line. A count shows how many cards fall on each side.
- An uncertain cheap card is never guessed. It stays unlisted, it is counted, and it keeps its slot.
- The Vault shows photo, price and location for each high-value card or slab, with a single confirm.
**Risk:** A valuable card misread as cheap sits unlisted unseen. The pile needs one periodic look, sorted by highest market price.

**3. Name: Checks must earn their place**
**The idea:** Each safety check and setting shows when it last fired or was changed. Anything silent for a season, guarding a harm smaller than the attention it costs, is proposed for deletion. It is replaced by one weekly line: "0 lost cards, 0 wrong pulls, 0 bad listings."
**Who it helps:** The owner, and everyone who reads warnings. Fewer alarms means the real ones get read.
**Why now:** "Many small safety checks" at 3 orders a day means most of them are guarding events that almost never happen.
**Rough shape:**
- One page lists each check with "last fired" and "harm it stops", sorted by silence.
- Each row has Delete and Keep buttons. Keep asks for a one-line reason.
- The weekly outcome line becomes the only standing signal.
**Risk:** A check that guards a rare but expensive event (a slab mis-shipped) looks idle. A harm floor must keep it whatever it costs.

**What must stay:** physical location for every card, identification that never guesses, and reporting unmatched rows when order files come back.

**Bet:** I would merge in direction 1 first. The Fulfiller's attention is the scarcest and least replaceable in the business. The merge deletes screens without deleting data, and it can be undone if it fails. Direction 2 saves more owner time but needs the value line tuned on real sales first.
