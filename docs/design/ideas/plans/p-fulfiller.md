# p-fulfiller

**The Fulfiller's day: three directions**

**1. Name:** One walk, neighbour-checked pulls
**The idea:** All of the day's orders become one route through the boxes, in box, section and position order. At each stop the Fulfiller sees the rig's photo of that exact copy, plus the cards on either side of it. They confirm the spot by matching pictures, not by reading set codes or printing names. Each pulled card goes into a numbered tray slot, and the cards are sorted into orders at the bench afterwards.
**Who it helps:** The Fulfiller most of all. The owner gets fewer wrong-printing shipments.
**Why now:** The rig already records position and a photo for every card. The pull is where "know where every card is" proves true or false. At 39 orders, a pick list per order means walking the boxes 39 times.
**Rough shape:**
- The iPad shows big photos: the card before, the target card, the card after. It also says "Box 3, after the Fossil divider, 12th card."
- The Fulfiller taps "Got it" and is told which tray slot to use: "Put in slot 7."
- A busy day is the same walk, only longer. A progress strip shows boxes done and boxes left.
**Risk:** Pulling a card moves the positions behind it, so the store must update them as cards leave, or the neighbours drift.

**2. Name:** Hesitation becomes a flag, never a decision
**The idea:** Every doubt the Fulfiller has (card not there, looks damaged, different printing, slab not found) gets one big button: "Something's off." They take a photo and keep going. The system suggests where a misfiled card most likely is, such as the next few spots or the matching spot in the next section. Anything that needs a call (refund, substitute, cancel) goes to the owner's queue. The Fulfiller never has to judge a price or condition.
**Who it helps:** The Fulfiller, who stops feeling at fault. The owner, who gets each exception with its photo.
**Why now:** With one non-technical helper, a single stuck card can stop the whole session. The rule "never guess" should protect the person at the box too, not only the software.
**Rough shape:**
- One flag button, three plain choices, an optional photo, then the next stop.
- "Try here first" shows the likely misfile spots as photos.
- A flagged order is held in its own tray slot, and the order's screen shows that it is waiting on the owner.
**Risk:** If the owner answers flags slowly, held orders pass the marketplace's ship-by deadline.

**3. Name:** The day card and the sealed proof
**The idea:** Before the walk, one card sets out the day. It shows the number of orders and cards, which envelope type each order needs (plain, toploader mailer, tracked), how many supplies to lay out, and which slabs or high-value cards need extra care. At the bench, each high-value envelope gets a quick photo of its open contents next to the order label before sealing. The day closes with "39 shipped, 0 misses", which is a record the Fulfiller can be proud of.
**Who it helps:** The Fulfiller, who gets a calm start and a clear finish. The owner, who has evidence when a buyer says the wrong card arrived.
**Why now:** A busy day feels different mostly because of setup and doubt, not the walking. A known count and supplies laid out first make 39 orders feel like 3. Disputes over a few costly cards outweigh hundreds of cheap ones.
**Rough shape:**
- Morning card: totals, supply counts by envelope type, and a "handle with care" list.
- Bench view: one order at a time. Its tray slot lights up, and the matching envelope type is named.
- Proof photo only above a value line the owner sets. An end-of-day summary and a streak.
**Risk:** The proof photo turns into a chore unless it stays limited to the costly few.

**Bet:** Build "One walk, neighbour-checked pulls" first. It pays off the system's main promise at the exact moment the Fulfiller is most likely to hesitate, and it reuses photos and positions the rig already records.
