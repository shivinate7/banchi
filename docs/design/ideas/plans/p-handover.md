# p-handover

Premises (source: brief only; I did not read the repo, as the brief asked, so I could not check any of them):
- Each card's box, section and position is recorded at capture (brief): unknown.
- No one can reach the owner while they are away (brief): unknown.
- Some cards are kept outside the long boxes, such as slabs and valuable cards (inferred): unknown.
- Orders come in as exported files, so a stand-in must import them (brief): unknown.

**1. Name:** Leave-ready check
**The idea:** Before the owner leaves, the store lists everything that only the owner knows. That means cards not in their recorded slot (slabs, valuable cards in a drawer, cards pulled for photos or returns), dividers with no printed label, half-finished orders, and supplies too low for a busy day. The check passes when a stranger could find every listed card.
**Who it helps:** The stand-in, who cannot tell a wrong record from their own mistake. The owner, who can leave without worrying.
**Why now:** A stranger trusts the record completely. Any gap the owner fills from memory turns into a cancelled order on day three.
**Rough shape:**
- One page shows "N listed cards a stranger could not find", grouped by cause, each with a fix.
- It prints divider tags and box-end labels that use the same box and section names as the pick screen.
- The store names 20 random slots and the owner checks each one in under a minute.
- A supplies line compares sleeves, top-loaders, envelopes and postage against a 39-order day.
**Risk:** A long list the night before a trip gets skipped, so it must be ready to run a week ahead.

**2. Name:** Rehearsal day on real stock
**The idea:** The store makes 10-15 practice orders from real stock, mixed like a real day: one slab, one card listed twice, one order with several cards. The stand-in pulls the cards, packs at the bench and puts them back. The store marks each pull right or wrong and confirms each card went back to its slot.
**Who it helps:** A new Fulfiller in their first week. The owner, who sees where a stranger hesitates.
**Why now:** Today the first real order is the training. One wrong card costs a buyer, a return and some seller rating.
**Rough shape:**
- "Start practice day" creates the orders. They never reach TCGplayer.
- The pick and pack screens are the same as on a real day, with PRACTICE marked on every one.
- An end report lists wrong pulls, slow sections and cards not put back.
- The owner can run it again with a harder mix, up to a 39-order peak day.
**Risk:** A practice card put back in the wrong slot spoils the real record, so the put-back must be checked.

**3. Name:** Calls made before leaving
**The idea:** A stand-in runs into only a few kinds of trouble: a missing card, a card in worse condition than listed, two orders for one copy, a damaged slab, a buyer message. The owner answers each kind once, ahead of time, with a price cut-off. The store shows that answer when the problem happens and logs it for the owner's return.
**Who it helps:** A stand-in with no one to ask. Buyers, who get a quick answer instead of two weeks of silence.
**Why now:** The owner may be away two weeks, and TCGplayer counts late shipments and cancellations against the seller.
**Rough shape:**
- On "can't find it", the screen searches nearby slots first, then applies the owner's rule. Under $X it cancels with a prepared message. Over $X it holds the order and flags it.
- Prepared replies cover the common buyer questions.
- Away mode can lengthen handling time or put less stock on sale, so a peak day fits one person's pace.
- A one-screen daily summary the owner can read on a phone, with nothing to answer.
**Risk:** A rule set too loose turns every hard pull into a cancellation.

**Bet:** Build the Leave-ready check first. The other two only work if the record is true. A stranger cannot tell a wrong slot from their own mistake, and only the owner can fix the record, and only before leaving. It is also the cheapest, because it reads data the store already has.
