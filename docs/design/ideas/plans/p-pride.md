# p-pride

**Fulfiller's Eye**
- **The idea:** The Fulfiller is the only person who looks at each card before the buyer does. Give them one tap to say "not as listed" (wrong variant, a crease, a sleeve swap), which holds that order line for the owner. Their judgement is treated as the last inspection, and each one shows as a catch, never as a miss.
- **Who it helps:** The Fulfiller, whose skill becomes part of the quality check. The owner, whose zero-attention listing gets a human backstop at the one moment it counts.
- **Why now:** Bulk listing means nobody looks at a card between the photo and the sale. As volume grows, so does the cost of a wrong card in an envelope.
- **Rough shape:**
  - On the pick screen, next to "Pulled", there is a quiet "Doesn't match" button with three or four plain reasons and an optional photo.
  - The owner sees the catch beside the original capture photo, then relists, reprices or refunds.
  - A running "caught before it shipped" line credits the Fulfiller by their chosen name.
- **Risk:** If every catch feels like extra work for the owner, the Fulfiller may stop flagging to spare them.

**Proof of Care**
- **The idea:** For valuable orders and slabs, the Fulfiller takes one photo of the card in its toploader and one of the sealed envelope before shipping. The photo belongs to the order and protects them in any "damaged" or "wrong card" dispute. Buyer reviews that mention packing are linked back to the parcel they packed, so the person who earned the kind words sees them.
- **Who it helps:** The Fulfiller, who has evidence on their side instead of being doubted. The owner, who wins disputes and sees what the buyer experienced.
- **Why now:** A few valuable cards carry most of the dispute risk. One lost slab claim costs more than months of cheap-card margin.
- **Rough shape:**
  - The camera opens only for orders over a value line the owner sets. It takes two shots, then "Sealed".
  - In a dispute, the owner opens the order and sees the photos with their times next to the capture photo.
  - The Fulfiller has a "kind words" page showing buyer reviews next to the parcel each one is about.
- **Risk:** If it widens past high-value orders, it starts to feel like checking up on them.

**The Well-Kept Box**
- **The idea:** Show each long box as a physical strip: sections, dividers, gaps where sold cards left, and crowding where new stock arrived. Suggest a short tidy now and then (move a divider, merge a thinned section) so the boxes stay as orderly as the records. The Fulfiller keeps the boxes, and the owner sees the inventory as one well-made object.
- **Who it helps:** The Fulfiller, whose pulls stay fast because the boxes do not drift. The owner, who can see the state of the whole stock in one look.
- **Why now:** With 545-732 cards per box and steady sales, sections thin out unevenly. Without a reset, "box, section, position" slowly stops matching the hand.
- **Rough shape:**
  - Six horizontal strips, one per box, each showing its sections to scale and its gaps lightly marked.
  - A suggested 10-minute tidy appears when a section drops below a threshold. The Fulfiller does it and taps "Done", and the record follows.
  - The Fulfiller can mark a damaged divider or a box that needs replacing.
- **Risk:** A position renumbered after a tidy could break an existing order's location if the record and the box fall out of step.

**Bet:** Fulfiller's Eye. It is used on every order, not only the rare valuable ones. It costs one button. It turns the single human look per card into the check that the photo, identify and price steps cannot make. It also says plainly that the person holding the card is trusted to judge it.
