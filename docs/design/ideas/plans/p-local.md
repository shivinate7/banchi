# p-local

**Three directions for selling outside the marketplace**

**1. Away Case: a location that travels**
**The idea:** A show binder, a slab case or a bag for trade night becomes a box in the store, with slots like the long boxes. Pulling a card into it takes the card off the next listing file. Selling it at the table is one tap on its photo, and that takes it out of stock everywhere.
**Who it helps:** The owner at a table. The Fulfiller at home, who finds out at once that an online order's card is "at the show with the owner," so they stop searching the boxes.
**Why now:** Listings go up as spreadsheet files, so an online listing can be hours behind a sale at the table. Without this, the first in-person sale risks selling the same card twice.
**Rough shape:**
- The owner chooses "pack for Saturday" and picks by set, by price or by slabs. The screen gives a pull list in box and section order.
- On the iPad at the table, they search or scan, tap Sold and enter the cash price.
- That night, "Come home" produces the list of cards to put back in their old box, section and slot, plus the listing file that adjusts quantities.
- If an online order arrives for a card in the Away Case, the owner's phone shows a prompt to hold it back.
**Risk:** Between pulling the card and the next upload, the online listing is still live. The owner may need to accept that window or pull the listings early.

**2. Decklist Pull for Locals**
**The idea:** Friends and the local store's players paste a decklist and see which cards the owner has, at a "local price." That price is the market price minus the fees and shipping that an online sale would lose. Reserving a card holds it out of the online listing until trade night.
**Who it helps:** Players, who get cards cheaper and the same week. The owner, who keeps margin the marketplace would take. The Fulfiller, who packs nothing.
**Why now:** Most of the stock is cheap cards. On those, fees and shipping take a large share of the price, so a sale in person nets more per card.
**Rough shape:**
- A QR code on a card at the local store opens a read-only page. The player pastes their list and sees "in stock: 31 of 60."
- They tap Reserve, which holds the cards for 7 days. The owner gets one pull list sorted by box.
- At hand-off, the owner taps Picked up, or the reservation expires and the cards list online again.
**Risk:** A public page reaches the store on the home network, so the store needs a safe read-only mirror. No-shows also lock cards away from online buyers.

**3. Below-the-Fee-Line Binder**
**The idea:** Banchi marks every card whose online sale nets almost nothing after fees, a sleeve, postage and the Fulfiller's minutes. It routes those cards to a "dollar binder" or a bulk box for the local store, not to the marketplace. Binder pages become locations.
**Who it helps:** The Fulfiller most. The low-value orders that net almost nothing stop taking their time.
**Why now:** Orders are mostly cheap cards, so most of the Fulfiller's time goes to cards worth less than that time.
**Rough shape:**
- The owner sets a "net floor" slider and sees how many cards sit under it and what they are worth.
- One press moves them out of the listing file and into a binder pull list.
- The binder counts as stock. A sale or a trade-in-kind at the store removes the cards.
**Risk:** Card prices move. A card under the floor today can spike later, so the binder needs to be re-priced on a schedule.

**Bet:** Away Case. It solves the one real danger of selling in two places, selling the same card twice, and it builds on what Banchi already does well: knowing where each card is. The other two directions both need a way to hold a card out of the online listing, and the Away Case builds that hold first.
